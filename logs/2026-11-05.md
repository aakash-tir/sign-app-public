# 2026-11-05

## Classifier bake-off: baseline vs TCN vs hand-shape features vs quantised ONNX (#64)

**What we did**
- Added an `ml/experiments/` harness: frozen fold splits, multi-seed cross-validation
  (`bakeoff/run_cv.py`), ONNX export and quantisation (`bakeoff/quantize.py`), an
  in-browser latency benchmark (`bench/`) and an aggregator (`bakeoff/aggregate.py`).
- Ran the shipped BiGRU recipe and two candidates over 5 signer-disjoint folds × 3 seeds
  (15 runs each), the letter MLP over 5 stratified folds × 3 seeds, and fp16 / int8
  variants of every fold model.
- Wrote up the results in `ml/experiments/RESULTS.md`, with the raw figures in
  `ml/experiments/results.json`.
- Result: the **TCN beats the baseline**, with top-1 0.741 against 0.698 (+0.043, 95% CI
  [+0.028, +0.058], ahead in 15 of 15 paired runs). Macro-F1 goes from 0.665 to 0.711.
  Hand-shape features are no better (+0.006, CI spans 0). The TCN with int8 weights is
  156 KB (the shipped model is 698 KB) and loses no accuracy. Every model runs in under
  1.5 ms p95 per prediction in WASM.
- No shipped model changed: nothing under `frontend/public/models` or
  `frontend/src/recognition/models` was touched.

**The reason**
The Phase-4 accuracy track had run out of recipe levers (P4M8 SSL came out flat), so
the owner asked for a controlled comparison of different architectures and features.
Every candidate used the same folds, seeds, metrics and recipe, so the differences come
from the model alone.

**How it was solved**
- The folds come from the shipped `signer_folds` call and are frozen to disk, with a hash
  of the cache, so every run uses identical splits. The harness reproduces the shipped
  CV number (0.695 against the recorded 0.691).
- The decision rule was written into `aggregate.py` before any candidate result
  existed: a candidate beats the baseline only with a mean paired gain above +0.01 whose
  95% t-interval excludes 0. No figure is a best fold.
- The first training attempt ran all 9 runs at once and was killed for low memory. The
  rerun memory-maps the data, runs one seed at a time, and can resume from the per-fold
  checkpoints.
- Weight-only int8 (per-channel `DequantizeLinear`) works on the GRU, which ORT's
  `quantize_dynamic` leaves unquantised. On the TCN, `quantize_dynamic` is 4× slower in
  WASM, so weight-only int8 is the variant to use.
- Training used the CPU-only PyTorch build, about 5 h in total.
- The harness was first written at the repo root. The owner moved it under `ml/`,
  because the root only holds the two workspaces and `tools/`. The paths in
  `common.py` and `bench.mjs` went one level deeper, and a rerun of `aggregate.py` from
  the new place gave a byte-identical `results.json`. Being under `ml/` means the `ml`
  CI lane now lints these scripts too. They were brought in line with ml's ruff config
  using format and import-sort changes only. `mypy .` picks them up as well, so
  `onnxruntime` joined the untyped-package overrides in `ml/pyproject.toml`. That's a
  config-only change, and the lockfile is unchanged.
- No `sign_ml` or `frontend/` code changed and no shipped model changed, so the
  architecture diagram doesn't change. Shipping the TCN is left for the owner to decide,
  in a separate issue.

## Ship the TCN as the dynamic-sign model (#66)

**What we did**
- `sign_ml` gained `--arch tcn` on `train` / `crossval` / `calibrate` (`build_tcn_model`,
  a `build_model` dispatcher; the BiGRU stays the `--arch gru` default) and `train --int8`
  (per-channel int8 weight-only ONNX). `calibrate` now saves its honest per-gloss report.
- Trained the TCN on the shipped 114-gloss `combined` cache with the shipped recipe, then
  re-calibrated it. The results replace `frontend/public/models/dynamic/model.onnx` and
  `metadata.json`.
- Honest signer-disjoint CV **0.691 → 0.763** (top-5 0.875 → 0.905). Published signs ≥ 0.50
  go from 95 to 98 of 103. The model goes 698 KB → 156 KB. No demotions.
- Filed #67 for the six unpublished signs that now clear the gate.

**The reason**
The #64 bake-off showed the TCN beats the BiGRU (+4.3 top-1, 15 of 15 paired runs), and
the owner approved the swap.

**How it was solved**
- **Kept the runtime contract:** the TCN takes the same `sequence` 30×161 input and gives
  the same `logits` 114 output, so the app's loader needed no change. The new metadata
  differs only in its per-sign accept bars.
- **Kept the shipped artifacts safe:** training ran in a copy of the cache
  (`ml/models/combined-tcn`), so the shipped BiGRU's files stayed intact.
- **Memory stops:** Claude Code stopped the background run twice when the machine ran low
  on memory. Train and calibrate were then run as separate steps, and the second attempt
  at each went through.
- **Gate:** per-sign comparison of all 103 published signs against the P4M17 BiGRU record:
  77 up, 12 down. `bus` is the only one that newly falls below 0.50 (0.53 → 0.47, about
  2 of 36 clips), and it's kept under the no-demote-on-noise policy.
- **Browser check:** in headless Chromium with the app's onnxruntime-web build, the int8
  model's top-1 on 20 real held-out windows matched onnxruntime CPU exactly.
- **Open question:** the pipeline gain (+7.2) is larger than the bake-off's controlled one
  (+4.3). That's recorded as a backlog item, and +4.3 is quoted as the conservative figure.
- **Architecture diagram:** updated (model labels, size, ADR-030) and screenshot-checked.
- **Backlog:** the `school` item is removed, since the TCN takes `school` to 0.80.
